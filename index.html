Log 00Bearing

A control engineer who keeps ending up near water.

Born in a village near Bodhgaya. Schooled at Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalaya. Still surprised by boats.

I am a Ph.D. research scholar in the Department of Aerospace Engineering at IIT Bombay, advised by Prof. Shashi Ranjan Kumar and Prof. Hemendra Arya. My thesis sits in the guidance and control of autonomous vehicles — most of it on unmanned surface vessels, where the plant is sluggish, underactuated, and permanently arguing with the sea.

The through-line of my work is constraint. A vessel must reach a berth at a particular angle, within a particular time, without asking its thrusters for more than they can give, and without wandering outside a corridor it was never allowed to leave. Barrier Lyapunov functions, true-proportional-navigation, and input magnitude-and-rate limits are the tools; the test is whether the thing still docks when the water is moving.

Before the Ph.D. I spent two and a half years building systems that had to survive contact with hardware: flight models and control laws for a manoeuvrable aerial target and a tilt-rotor at L&T Precision Engineering & Systems, deployed onto an actual flight control computer and shaken out through hardware-in-the-loop testing; and before that, vital-sign estimation from Doppler radar at Sleepiz, pulling breathing and heart rate out of noise while someone slept three feet away.

That order — hardware first, then theory — is why I care less about elegant proofs than about whether a controller degrades gracefully. I work in MATLAB/Simulink, Python, C++ and ROS-Gazebo, and I would rather spend a week on a bench test than a day on a nicer bound.

Current lines of work

Fig. 1 — approach-angle constrained terminal geometry

Thread AGuidance for unmanned surface vessels

Terminal guidance laws that deliver a surface vessel to a target with a specified approach angle, and separately with a specified time of arrival, built on true-proportional-navigation. The interesting part is that a USV cannot slew like a missile: the same geometry that makes PN elegant in air becomes a genuinely constrained problem on water.

  • TPN
  • Impact angle
  • Time constraint
  • Underactuated

Fig. 2 — motion confined by a barrier Lyapunov certificate

Thread BBarrier Lyapunov control under motion constraints

A controller that keeps a surface vessel's states inside prescribed bounds for all time — not as a soft penalty, but as a structural property of the Lyapunov function itself. Useful when the constraint is a channel, a safety envelope, or a sensor's field of view rather than a preference.

  • Barrier Lyapunov function
  • State constraints
  • Stability proof

Fig. 3 — command vs. magnitude- and rate-limited actuation

Thread CTrajectory tracking with input magnitude & rate limits

Thrusters have a maximum output and a maximum slew. Ignore the second and a controller that looks fine in simulation will chatter a real propulsion system to pieces. This line designs tracking control that respects both, in the presence of environmental disturbance, and proves the resulting closed loop still converges.

  • Saturation
  • Rate limits
  • Disturbance rejection

Fig. 4 — rendezvous with a non-stationary berth

Thread DAutonomous docking with stationary & moving ships

The hardest version of the problem: the berth itself is under way. Docking becomes a rendezvous with terminal position, heading and relative-velocity conditions all binding at once — and the approach corridor closes as you get near. Currently in preparation.

  • Docking
  • Rendezvous
  • Moving target
